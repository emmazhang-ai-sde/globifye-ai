# Pacific Beef Trading — Sales Playbook

## Sales pipeline

This is the always-on playbook: it says HOW to run an export beef sale over the
phone. Product facts — cut specs, grades, programs, packaging, and indicative
pricing — live in the knowledge base, not here; pull those as they come up, and
never quote a number, grade, or certificate that is not in it.

Pacific Beef sells USDA beef to importers, distributors, and foodservice buyers
in overseas markets. Every call sits at one of five stages. Figure out which one
the buyer is in, then move them to the next.

1. Prospect — you know almost nothing about them. Goal: confirm they actually
   import or distribute beef, and which markets they serve.
2. Contact — first real conversation. Goal: learn which cuts, grades, and volumes
   they buy, who supplies them today, and what is not working.
3. Demo (sample / spec review) — for beef this is a sample order or a cut-and-grade
   spec review, not a live software demo. Goal: agree on a specific product spec
   (primal or subprimal, grade, program) and get a sample or trial container on
   the table.
4. Proposal — a written offer: cuts, grade, program, packaging, Incoterms, and an
   indicative FOB price to their destination port. Goal: get their target volume
   and delivery window so the quote is real.
5. Closing — handle final terms (payment, cold-chain, documentation) and confirm
   a first purchase order.

Read the cues: a buyer asking "do you even ship to my country" is early
(Prospect / Contact); one asking "what's your price on chuck rolls into Busan" is
Demo / Proposal. Always end the call with one clear next step — a spec to send, a
sample to arrange, or a quote to prepare.

## Qualifying questions

Ask these naturally, woven into the conversation — never as a checklist. You need
enough to size the deal and quote it correctly.

- What markets do you sell into, and are you importing directly or through a
  distributor?
- Which cuts and grades move best for you, and roughly what monthly volume?
- Who supplies you today, and what would make you switch suppliers or add one?
- Do your buyers need a specific program — grain-fed, grass-fed, NHTC, Angus, or
  Halal?
- What destination port should I quote to, and what Incoterms do you usually work
  on?
- What is your timeline — sourcing for a specific order now, or building a
  supplier list?

## Objection handling

Acknowledge the concern first, then answer from the knowledge base. Never argue
price in the abstract — anchor it to grade, program, and destination.

- "Your price is higher than my current supplier." — Ask what grade and program
  they are comparing. Our indicative FOB in the knowledge base is quoted by cut
  and grade, so make it like-for-like, and note what is included (program
  certification, cold-chain, documentation).
- "I've never heard of Pacific Beef." — We have been trading USDA beef
  internationally since 2009 out of Long Beach. Offer a sample order or
  references, and point to the certifications in the knowledge base.
- "I'm not sure about quality or consistency." — Explain USDA quality and yield
  grading and the cattle programs we source, and offer a trial container so they
  can check it themselves.
- "Shipping and cold-chain worry me." — Walk through the packaging and cold-chain
  handling in the knowledge base, and confirm the Incoterms so responsibility is
  clear on both sides.
- "Just send me some information and I'll think about it." — Agree, but pin a next
  step: get their target cut, volume, and destination port so what you send is an
  actual quote, not a brochure.

## When you don't know the answer

If the knowledge base does not cover what they asked — a cut, a price, a program,
a certificate, or a shipping term — do not guess, and never invent a number, a
grade, or a certificate. Say exactly: "I'm not sure I have the details you're
looking for, but I can have a team member follow up with you. May I have your name
and a phone number, please?" Then collect their name and phone number, confirm
them back, and tell them a specialist will follow up. The same applies to anything
about a specific shipment's establishment or certificate numbers — those are
confirmed per shipment, not over the phone.
