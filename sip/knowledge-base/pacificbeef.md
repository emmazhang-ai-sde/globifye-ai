# Pacific Beef Trading — Sales Knowledge Base

This document is the single reference for anyone selling for Pacific Beef, whether an AI sales representative or a new human rep in training. Read the overview and "how we operate" first to understand the business, then use the catalog, terms, FAQ, and playbook sections to answer buyers and move a deal forward. Tone with buyers: professional, straightforward, trade-savvy.

## Company overview (what we do)
Pacific Beef Trading is a U.S. beef exporter, headquartered near the Port of Long Beach, California, and trading since 2009. In one sentence: **we are the link between American beef packers and overseas buyers.** We buy USDA-inspected beef from FSIS-registered packing plants across the U.S. Midwest and Plains, consolidate and cold-store it near port, and export it, chilled or frozen, to importers, distributors, and food-service buyers around the world.

What that means concretely: an overseas buyer can order a container of a specific cut, grade, and program (for example, Choice grade striploin, grain-fed, frozen), and we handle everything between the U.S. plant and their port, the sourcing, grade selection, export packing, cold-chain shipping, and every export document, so it arrives certified and ready to clear customs.

Important distinction for reps: **we are a trading and export company, not a slaughter or packing plant.** We do not own cattle, feedlots, or kill floors. Our value is range (the full carcass, three grades, multiple programs), consistency, correct paperwork for each market, and reliable cold-chain delivery. When a buyer asks "are you a packer?", the honest answer is: we are an exporter who sources from USDA-inspected packers.

Scale, in round numbers: we ship roughly 40 to 60 containers a month to more than 15 markets, led by South Korea, Japan, Taiwan, and the Middle East. A typical order is one or more full-container loads (about 25 metric tons each) of a defined cut, grade, and program, on agreed Incoterms.

## How we operate (sourcing to delivery)
Every order moves through the same six steps. Knowing them lets a rep answer "how does this actually work?"
1. **Source** — we buy to the buyer's spec from USDA-inspected, FSIS-registered plants, selecting the grade and program (grain-fed, grass-fed, NHTC, Halal, under-30-month, etc.).
2. **Consolidate and cold-store** — boxed beef is held chilled or frozen at a port-adjacent cold store while the load is built.
3. **Export pack** — cuts are vacuum-packed and boxed, cartons labeled with product, grade, plant establishment number, weight, and dates, then palletized.
4. **Document** — we obtain the USDA FSIS export health certificate plus the commercial and market-specific paperwork (see Export documentation).
5. **Ship** — the load is loaded into a reefer container at temperature and booked on an ocean service to the buyer's port, temperature monitored end to end.
6. **Support** — we provide tracking, reefer data on request, and follow up through delivery.

## Who we sell to

- Typical contacts: purchasing managers, import managers, category buyers, and distributor owners.

## What we sell
We carry the full carcass: every primal and the common export subprimals, plus variety meats and lean trimmings, in USDA grades Prime, Choice, and Select. Chilled (vacuum-packed) or frozen, grain-fed or grass-fed, with Halal and other program certifications on request. The catalog below is organized by primal.

## Product catalog: primal and subprimal cuts
Eight primals, with the subprimals buyers ask for most:
- **Chuck** (forequarter): chuck roll, chuck eye roll, shoulder clod, chuck short rib, chuck tender.
- **Rib**: ribeye roll (cube roll), bone-in rib, ribeye lip-on, back ribs.
- **Loin**: striploin (short loin), tenderloin (PSMO — peeled, side muscle on or off), top sirloin butt, bottom sirloin (tri-tip, ball tip, flap).
- **Round** (hindquarter): top (inside) round, bottom (outside) round / gooseneck, eye of round, knuckle (sirloin tip).
- **Brisket**: whole brisket, point (deckle) and flat.
- **Short plate**: short ribs, navel end, inside and outside skirt.
- **Flank**: flank steak.
- **Shank**: fore and hind shank meat, osso buco cross-cuts.

## Variety meats and trimmings
- **Variety meats (offal)**: liver, tongue, heart, tripe (honeycomb and flat), oxtail, cheek meat, mountain chain tripe. Strong demand in Asian and Latin American markets. Classified under HS 0206.
- **Lean trimmings for grinding**: 50CL, 65CL, 73CL, 81CL, 85CL, and 90CL (CL is the chemical-lean, or lean-to-fat, ratio). Sold frozen in combo bins or cartons.

## USDA quality and yield grades
- **Quality grades** (based on marbling and maturity, best to lowest): Prime, Choice, Select, then Standard, Commercial, Utility, Cutter, Canner. We export mainly Prime, Choice, and Select.
- **Yield grades** 1 to 5 measure the ratio of lean to fat and the expected boneless-cut yield: Yield Grade 1 is the leanest and highest-yielding, 5 the fattest.
- Prime has the most marbling and suits premium food service; Choice is the volume workhorse for most export programs; Select is leaner and price-sensitive.
- All beef is USDA-inspected for wholesomeness (mandatory). Grading into Prime, Choice, or Select is a separate, voluntary quality assessment by USDA graders.

## Cattle programs and certifications
- **Grain-fed**: the standard U.S. program, higher marbling.
- **Grass-fed**: leaner, program-verified, for buyers who require it.
- **NHTC (Non-Hormone Treated Cattle)**: required for the EU market, program-verified.
- **Angus and branded programs**: available for buyers who want a branded, higher-marbling spec.
- **Halal**: certified programs available on request for the Middle East and Muslim-majority markets.
- Program eligibility is verified through USDA AMS Quality System Assessment (QSA) and Process Verified Programs. Specific certificate and establishment numbers are plant- and shipment-specific and appear on the export paperwork; reps should not quote a number from memory.

## Packaging, chilling, and shelf life
- **Chilled**: individual cuts vacuum-packed (Cryovac), boxed, held at -1.5°C to 0°C. Shelf life for vacuum-packed chilled whole-muscle beef is typically 90 to 120 days with an unbroken cold chain.
- **Frozen**: blast-frozen and held at -18°C or colder (shipped at -20°C and below); shelf life 12 to 18 months.
- Cartons are labeled with product, grade, establishment number, net weight, and production and use-by dates.
- A 40-foot high-cube reefer container carries roughly 25 to 27 metric tons of boxed beef.

## Incoterms and shipping
- We quote most commonly **FOB** (U.S. West Coast port, for example Los Angeles / Long Beach, Oakland, or Tacoma), **CFR**, or **CIF** destination port (Incoterms 2020).
- FOB: risk passes to the buyer once the goods are loaded on the vessel; the buyer arranges main freight and insurance.
- CFR: we arrange and pay ocean freight to the destination port; risk still passes at loading.
- CIF: as CFR, plus we arrange marine insurance. Note that standard CIF cover is the minimum (Institute Cargo Clauses C); buyers who want reefer-breakdown or spoilage cover should ask for broader terms.
- We can also quote EXW, FCA, CIP, DAP, or DDP where a buyer needs it.

## Cold chain and container specifications
- Refrigerated (reefer) containers, set to the product's carriage temperature and monitored end to end.
- Chilled loads: delivery-air temperature held around -1.5°C to 0°C, never below about -1.4°C, to avoid freeze damage.
- Frozen loads: -18°C or colder.
- We supply temperature set-point instructions and can provide reefer data logs on request.
- Cold chain is the buyer's biggest risk in ocean beef trade; we load at temperature and document it.

## Export documentation
Every shipment ships with the paperwork the destination requires. The standard set:
- **USDA FSIS export health certificate** (FSIS Form 9060-5), issued through USDA's PHIS system, certifying the product and its establishment.
- **Certificate of origin**, **commercial invoice**, and **packing list**.
- **Bill of lading** from the ocean carrier.
- Market-specific certificates as required (for example a Halal certificate, EU eligibility, or country-specific health attestations).
- Our sourcing establishments carry FSIS establishment numbers and are listed as eligible in the FSIS Export Library for the destination country. The specific number is on the certificate for each shipment.

## Target markets and market access
- **South Korea and Japan**: require beef from cattle under 30 months of age, produced under an approved USDA AMS QSA / Export Verification program (the "LT30" program). We source eligible product for these markets.
- **China and Hong Kong**: served through eligible, registered establishments; requirements and plant registration vary, so confirm current status per order.
- **Middle East**: Halal-certified programs.
- **Latin America and Southeast Asia**: chilled and frozen programs by market.
- BSE-related rules (removal of Specified Risk Materials, age limits) are handled through the EV / QSA programs above. If a buyer's exact market rule is not covered here, do not guess — take their details for follow-up.

## Payment terms and lead times
- **Payment**: an irrevocable Letter of Credit (L/C) at sight is standard for new buyers; telegraphic transfer (T/T) or cash against documents (CAD) for established relationships; a deposit plus balance can be arranged.
- **Lead times**: frozen orders typically ship about 2 to 4 weeks after order confirmation and documentation; chilled programs are scheduled tighter to protect shelf life. Confirm exact timing in the proposal.

## Customer FAQ (common questions)
Short, direct answers to what buyers ask most. Use these as-is, then move the conversation to the next pipeline stage.
- **"Are you a packer or a trader?"** We are an exporter and trader. We source USDA-inspected beef from FSIS-registered packing plants; we do not own kill floors.
- **"What is your minimum order?"** We sell by the full container, roughly 25 metric tons. We can build mixed-cut loads to reach your volume.
- **"Chilled or frozen?"** Both. Chilled vacuum-packed whole muscle keeps about 90 to 120 days; frozen keeps 12 to 18 months.
- **"Which grades do you carry?"** Prime, Choice, and Select. Choice is the most common export grade; Prime is premium; Select is leaner and lower cost.
- **"Do you offer Halal?"** Yes, Halal-certified programs on request. Tell us the certifying body your market requires.
- **"Can you supply Korea or Japan?"** Yes. Those markets need under-30-month beef under the approved LT30 QSA / EV program, and we source eligible product.
- **"Which Incoterms do you work on?"** Most often FOB West Coast, CFR, or CIF. We can also quote others such as CIP, DAP, or DDP.
- **"How do you price?"** Per pound or per metric ton, and it moves with the market. We give ranges on the call and a firm quote in the written proposal.
- **"What are your payment terms?"** An irrevocable L/C at sight for new buyers; T/T or cash against documents for established relationships.
- **"How long from order to shipment?"** Frozen is usually about 2 to 4 weeks after confirmation; chilled is scheduled tighter to protect shelf life.
- **"What documents come with the shipment?"** USDA FSIS export health certificate (9060-5), certificate of origin, commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, and any market-specific certificates.
- **"Can we try before committing?"** Yes. We can arrange a trial container so you can prove the spec before a standing program.
- **"What is your establishment number?"** It is plant- and shipment-specific and appears on the export certificate; we confirm it per order rather than quoting one from memory.

## Trade glossary
Quick definitions a new rep should know, and can explain if a buyer asks.
- **FOB / CFR / CIF** — Incoterms 2020: who pays and bears risk for freight and insurance, and up to which point.
- **FSIS** — USDA's Food Safety and Inspection Service; inspects meat and issues the export health certificate.
- **USDA grade** — voluntary quality grade (Prime, Choice, Select) based on marbling and maturity.
- **EST number** — a plant's FSIS establishment number, printed on the carton and certificate.
- **QSA / EV / LT30** — USDA programs that verify product meets a market's rules; LT30 means cattle under 30 months, required by Korea and Japan.
- **NHTC** — Non-Hormone Treated Cattle, required for the EU.
- **SRM / BSE** — Specified Risk Materials, removed for BSE (mad cow) safety rules.
- **PSMO** — a tenderloin that is peeled, side muscle on.
- **CL** — chemical lean, the lean-to-fat percentage of trimmings (for example 90CL).
- **Reefer** — a refrigerated shipping container.
- **L/C, T/T, CAD** — Letter of Credit, telegraphic transfer (wire), cash against documents.
- **HS code** — the customs classification: 0201 chilled beef, 0202 frozen beef, 0206 offal.

## The sales pipeline (follow these stages)
Identify where the prospect is and move them to the next stage. Do not skip stages.

1. **Prospect** — the buyer may not know us. Goal: earn a first conversation. Qualify their market, the cuts and grades they need, and volume.
2. **Contact** — understand their needs: destination market, required cuts and grades, monthly volume, chilled or frozen, and import and cold-chain requirements. Goal: agree to review product specs.
3. **Demo** — send product specs, grade sheets, and a sample shipment if needed. Confirm cuts, grade, packaging, and certifications. Goal: agree on what to quote.
4. **Proposal** — quote price per cut and grade, volume, Incoterms, delivery timeline, and payment terms. Goal: reach terms and handle objections.
5. **Closing** — confirm the order, delivery schedule, documentation, and payment instrument; capture the buyer's name and contact. Goal: a signed purchase order or supply contract.

## Qualifying questions
- Which market are you importing into?
- Which cuts and grades do you need, and at what monthly volume?
- Grain-fed, grass-fed, NHTC, or Halal-certified?
- Chilled or frozen, and what packaging?
- Which Incoterms do you work on — FOB, CFR, or CIF?
- What are your import, documentation, and cold-chain requirements?
- What payment terms do you usually work on?

## Common objections
- "Your price is higher than another supplier." Tie price to grade consistency, USDA inspection and grading, reliable supply, and correct export paperwork. Offer a trial container to prove the spec.
- "Australian (or other) beef is cheaper." U.S. grain-fed marbling and Prime/Choice consistency command a premium; match the grade to their price point (Select, or specific cuts) rather than discounting the program.
- "We're not sure about supply reliability." Emphasize consistent container volume, multiple sourcing establishments, and full documentation; offer references and a first trial load.
- "Shipping and customs are complex." We handle export documentation and can quote CIF so the buyer's landed cost is clear; we supply temperature instructions and reefer data.
- "We're worried about cold-chain and spoilage." We load at temperature, document it, and can provide reefer data logs; note that CIF insurance is minimum cover and offer broader terms if they want spoilage protection.
- "Is it Halal?" Halal-certified programs are available on request; confirm the certifying body they require.
- "Can you meet our market's age or BSE rules?" For Korea and Japan we source under-30-month product through approved AMS QSA / EV programs; take their exact market and requirements for confirmation.
- "Your minimum order is too big." We sell by the container but can build mixed-cut loads to hit their volume; if it is still too small, take their details for follow-up.

## When you don't know
If the prospect asks something not covered here — a specific market's current import rules, a custom cut spec, plant registration status, a specific establishment or certificate number, or exact current pricing — do not guess. Say a team member will follow up, and take their name and phone number.
